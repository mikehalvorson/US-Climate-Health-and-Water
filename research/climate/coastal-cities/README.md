# Coastal-city sea-level risk evidence package

Machine-first evidence for New Orleans, Miami, displacement, receiving-city
capacity, and dashboard implementation. JSON and CSV artifacts are normative.

The package does **not** assign either city a single "underwater by" year. It
separates relative sea-level rise, permanent inundation, high-tide flooding,
rainfall flooding, groundwater rise, storm surge, engineered protection, and
functional habitability. Every projection retains its scenario, geography,
baseline, time horizon, and adaptation assumptions.

## Routing

- `claim-audit.json`: verified, qualified, and rejected headline statements.
- `city-risk-profiles.json`: hazard pathways and infrastructure dependencies.
- `displacement-and-destinations.json`: exposed-population and migration models.
- `receiving-city-strain.json`: evidence and indicators for destination cities.
- `adaptation-pathways.json`: protect, accommodate, avoid, restore, and relocate.
- `chart-contracts.json`: render rules that prevent unsupported visual claims.
- `gaps.json`: evidence needed before parcel-level or policy-specific claims.
- `source-verification.json`: three-check audits for central claims.
- `timeseries/`: normalized NOAA projections, observed trends, flood-day
  scenarios, migration stress tests, source hashes, and the reproducing script.

## Interpretation rule

The defensible public message is progressive and conditional: rising relative
sea level increases recurrent flooding and loads on drainage, water, wastewater,
transport, housing, insurance, and coastal defenses. A city may become harder or
more expensive to inhabit before every parcel is permanently inundated. The date
of such functional loss depends on emissions, land motion, storms, investment,
maintenance, governance, household resources, and the threshold being measured.
